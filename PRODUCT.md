# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Homeowners and residents in and around Atlanta who need a trustworthy local
business — a plumber, an electrician, a caterer — and are tired of sorting
through paid placements to find one. They arrive on a phone, usually with a
problem already in hand, and they are deciding whether to trust this app at all.

The second audience is the local pro: a small independent operator checking
whether they qualify for the Shmooze registry. Same screen, different job.

The home tab's job is not to complete a task. It is to make four distinct
entries legible in one scroll — Concierge (we'll email you recommendations),
Match (swipe to find a fit), the certified registry itself, and the community —
and to make the Shmooze feel like a place worth belonging to.

## Product Purpose

A vetted, human-curated registry of Southern local businesses, plus two ways in:
a concierge request form and a swipe-to-match deck. Success is a resident who
found someone good and would say so out loud to a neighbor.

The product is trust. Everything on the surface is either earning it or spending it.

## Brand Personality

Warm · Neighborly · Trustworthy.

Warm without costume. The voice is a neighbor who actually knows a guy, not a
marketplace bidding for the lead. Handwriting, peach, and floral texture are
garnish on a credible base, never the whole meal. Certification is the one place
the app is allowed to sound institutional, because that claim has to hold.

## Anti-references

**Yelp / Angi / Thumbtack.** The category reflex and the thing this exists to
replace: dense marketplace chrome, sponsored rows disguised as results, star
ratings as the only signal, and a layout optimized for extracting a lead rather
than answering a question. If the home screen ever reads as a lead-gen funnel,
it has failed.

Downstream of that: no ad-shaped blocks, no urgency pressure, no equal-weight
stack of interchangeable offers competing for the same tap.

## Design Principles

1. **Trust is earned by specificity.** "Certified" means something here, so show
   the substance behind it rather than decorating the badge.
2. **One clear front door per visit.** Four entries exist, but they are not peers.
   The screen should make the primary path obvious and let the rest recede.
3. **Warmth in the material, not the costume.** Texture, type, and color carry
   the Southern read. No mason jars, no chalkboard script.
4. **Never look like a marketplace.** When a pattern would be at home on Angi,
   pick a different one.
5. **The registry is the product; the app is the porch.** Surfaces that lead to
   real vetted businesses outrank surfaces that promote the app itself.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG 2.2 AA as the working bar. Body text ≥4.5:1, large text ≥3:1, 44×44pt
minimum touch targets (already enforced — a11y review, PR #35). Screen-reader
labels on every tappable block, including the promo banners where the whole
surface is one target. Reduced-motion honored for any motion added later.
Assume outdoor daylight phone use: contrast has real headroom, not just passing
headroom.
